### BEAT 2 — Stochastic lineage `[CAM]` + graphics (0:55–2:20)

**Say:**

> Large language models feel like oracles. They aren't. They're **stochastic systems** — a fancy way of saying they're machines that work in probabilities, not certainties.
>
> **Stochastic** means: given the same input, you get a **distribution** of possible outputs, not one guaranteed answer. The model rolls weighted dice on what word probably comes next. Plausible. Not necessarily true.
>
> That lineage is older than Silicon Valley hype. In the nineteen-twenties, **Agner Erlang** modeled telephone traffic for the Copenhagen phone company — calls arriving at random times, finite trunks, blocking when lines are full. The public phone network had to be engineered for uncertainty before anyone said "artificial intelligence."
>
> In nineteen forty-eight, **Claude Shannon** at Bell Labs treated communication itself as statistical. His employer built the infrastructure Erlang's math described.
>
> In nineteen-sixties Britain, the Post Office rolled out electronic exchanges — **TXE** — while at **Standard Telephones and Cables**, **Brian Gaines** pioneered **stochastic computing**: processors that could learn, built on probability as a computational primitive. Telephone infrastructure forced stochastic thinking. Gaines turned it into a research program.
>
> Fast-forward: n-gram language models predict the next word from the last few words. Neural nets. Transformers. ChatGPT. Same job — predict likely continuations — at a scale that made the public notice.
>
> The model does not retrieve a fact. It samples from a probability cloud. That's why it hallucinates. That's why the same prompt gives different answers. That's why unconstrained "fix my chapter" produces generic prose. **The engine is stochastic. Governance is not optional.**

>

If that pipeline runs on Whisper or Chirp, your draft already crossed the same vendor families. You spoke; you revise; you own the file. That's capture and edit — not ChatGPT ghostwriting — but it's not zero AI either.

> The argument isn't that every app is ChatGPT. It's that you already **trust or reject probabilistic machine outputs** every day — and twenty-twenty-two made that visible for **text**.
>

>
> Accept an autocomplete suggestion and you already practice **human-in-the-loop selection** from a machine retrieval set. The methodology serious writers use scales that instinct to long-form craft. It does not introduce alien behavior.
