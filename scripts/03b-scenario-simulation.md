## Hook (0:00–0:15)

> Geography and play-pretend are not the same mental move. One asks *where*; the other asks *who under pressure*. Collapsing them breaks workflows.

**On-screen:** `Maps ≠ pretend`

---

## Beat 1 — The human split (0:15–1:00)

**Say:**

> Level five-a: grounding — spatial relations, routes, period material facts. You verify before you write. Level five-b: scenario simulation — you run a character or institution **off-page**, like table-top roleplay for craft. The output is constraints and knowledge state — not dialogue you paste into chapter four.

**On-screen:** `5a verify` | `5b play-pretend`

---

## Beat 2 — Same promotion gate (1:00–1:45)

**Say:**

> Both start in exploration — non-canonical scratch. Both promote through **you**: routes to lore files, simulation distillates to character PGMs. Domain collapse is pasting sandbox chat into the manuscript, or treating a map query like a character session.

**On-screen:** `exploration → promote → canon`

---

## Beat 3 — What simulation produces (1:45–2:30)

**Say:**

> Good simulation ends with: what she concludes, what she files, what she refuses to file, operational constraints for drafting. Bad simulation ends with: a scene you didn't write. You still own the prose mass.

**On-screen:** `Constraints · not chapter paste`
