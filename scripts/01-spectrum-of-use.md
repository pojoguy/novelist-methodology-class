## Hook (0:00–0:10)

> "Write me a bestseller" is level zero. Serious authors operate at level two through seven — and most of that work can **start** in an ordinary chat tab, no special software.

**On-screen:** `Level 0 → Level 7` · `Start in frontier chat`

---

## Beat 1 — Level 0 vs everything else (0:10–0:50)

**Say:**

> Level zero is ghostwriting: accept the output, call it yours. Level one is brainstorming — lists and what-ifs, high discard rate. Levels two through seven add structure: six word options instead of one substitution, readonly developmental audit, continuity files you reload, grounding sandboxes, production blueprints, agentic workflows with gates. Most public debate is about level zero. This series is about the rest.

**On-screen:** Spectrum ladder (0–7)

**Visual:** Paired doc table or simple graphic from `docs/01`.

---

## Beat 2 — Frontier chat first (0:50–1:40)

**Say:**

> You do **not** need a plugin or a governed IDE to **learn** this. The methodology was prototyped in frontier chat — prosthetic habits, adversarial audit, and the **first PGM sheets** were all drafted there and saved by hand. Chat helps you shape canon; **you** save the file. What tooling adds later is registered lookup — when re-pasting your notes every session hurts, or when **arc density** on the page outgrows one sitting.

**On-screen:** `Chat → file you save` · `Tooling later`

---

## Beat 3 — Sales mode vs adversarial discipline (1:40–2:35)

**Say:**

> Frontier chat defaults to **please you** — flattery, eager rewrites. That is sycophantic audit. A store-bought "brutal editor" persona does not fix it — same habits, tougher label. **Adversarial discipline** means readonly critique: verdict, what's working briefly, numbered line-tied problems, priority order — and **you** revise. Spell out word contracts: *priority fixes* means finish the audit and offer options, not rewrite my chapter. Paste the letter template every pass. Fresh thread when you can.

**On-screen:** `Paste letter template` · `Priority fixes ≠ rewrite`

---

## Beat 4 — Lexical prosthetic and audit (2:35–3:20)

**Say:**

> Level two: you know the beat, you cannot retrieve the word. Six alternatives — inspiration, not a menu order. You pick, blend, reject, or write your own. Level three: adversarial editorial pass on **your** draft — no apply without your instruction. That is closer to spell-check plus a honest beta reader than to ghostwriting.

**On-screen:** `6 options → you decide` · `Audit · readonly`

---

## Beat 5 — PGMs and the honest scale limit (3:20–4:15)

**Say:**

> Level four is continuity — character sheets, who-knows-what, timeline slices. You can **build** those in chat and save them yourself. Chat will not **hold** the joins across months. Maintainer experience: about **one governed chapter per sitting** in frontier chat is the practical ceiling. A **half-page vignette** that resolves **several long-running arcs at once** can bog down sooner than a whole chapter of single-arc material. Tooling often arrives in **weeks** on a braided novel — not because you failed, but because relationship fidelity does not live in chat memory.

**On-screen:** `~1 chapter / session` · `Dense half-page = harder`

---

## Beat 6 — Sandboxes and agentic (4:15–4:45)

**Say:**

> Level five splits: five-a verify geography and material facts — maps, Street View, routes on file. Five-b play-pretend off-page — distill constraints, do not paste sandbox dialogue into the chapter. Level six is production blueprinting. Level seven is multi-step orchestration with gates — honestly needs filed rules and state; that is what a governed IDE documents.

**On-screen:** `5a maps` · `5b pretend` · `7 = orchestration`

---

## Beat 7 — Talking to skeptics (4:45–5:15)

**Say:**

> When someone says AI cannot write real books — agree, if they mean level zero. When someone says any AI use is cheating — ask which level. When someone says AI will replace authors — point to levels four through seven: designing methodology, maintaining state, authorizing every change. That is not less work. It is different work.

**On-screen:** `Which level?`
