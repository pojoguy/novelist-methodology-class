## Beat 3 — Adherence vs attribution (1:50–2:45)

**Say:**

> Commercial detectors don't measure who wrote the line. Under the hood: implicit pattern rubrics plus stats on training-material shapes. Signs are the same signal class — explicit, for craft, not sold as forensic attribution. Maintainer built a comparable rubric in hours; products wrap similar logic in an AI score. What gets measured is adherence. What gets mislabeled is authorship. Franchise formula — think Franklin W. Dixon under Simon and Schuster, beat and vocabulary on brief — can score AI when it's purely human, because formula adherence is the product. Voice-forward governed work? High unprompted adherence is often a craft problem. Detector culture punishes genre compliance and ghostwriting with the same score. On this corpus, explicit Signs beat every commercial detector at separating high-adherence prose from organically built governed work — that's adherence separation, not AI detection. N equals one. Wikipedia's pattern guide shares the stance: neither proves tool use.

**On-screen:** `Adherence ≠ attribution` · `Formula can false-positive` · `Signs = craft rubric`
