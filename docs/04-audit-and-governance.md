# Audit and Governance

**Domain 2** — **audit** (diagnose with Signs, letters, filed reports) and **governance** (apply gate, locks, word contracts). The split is explicit up front; most sections below are audit mechanics because diagnosis is long to learn — governance is shorter but non-negotiable. Prosthetic **move types** (pick vs cluster + author take), **six alternatives**, and **malformed-beat** exits: `[02-prosthetic-model.md](02-prosthetic-model.md)`. Failure catalog: `[06-failure-modes.md](06-failure-modes.md)`. Signs vs detectors: `[07-ethics-and-transparency.md](07-ethics-and-transparency.md)`.

## Terms in this document

**For non-technical readers:** plain-language definitions for jargon used below.  
**Full glossary:** `[glossary.md](glossary.md)`.


| Term | Plain language |
|------|----------------|
| **Audit** | A **diagnostic pass** on existing prose — what works, what fails, what to try next — without someone else rewriting your voice. |
| **Governance** | **Authorization** — apply gate, locks, and word contracts; nothing touches the manuscript until you say so. Not the same as having a rubric. See [*Audit vs governance*](#audit-vs-governance). |
| **Rubric / Sign** | A **craft vocabulary** for generic or model-typical patterns in your draft — name it, point at the line, you decide what to do. |
| **Editorial recommendations format** | The shape of a **good developmental letter** — verdict, what's working, numbered notes you can act on. |
| **Editorial report corpus** | **Filed critiques** you keep across months — trope ledger, tone pass, continuity checks — so the next session does not start from zero. |
| **Author lock** | A **rule you set and keep** (naming, calendar, voice) that outranks generic tool habits. |
| **Apply gate** | Nothing changes in the manuscript until **you** pick an option or name a specific line fix. Walkthrough: [*Apply gate in practice*](#apply-gate-in-practice). |
| **Prosthetic gates** | **Quote gate** (dialogue waits for speaker + situation), **tagline gate** (critique stays specific, not poster morals), **apply gate**, and **priority-fixes** meaning (critique + options, not paste-in rewrite). |
| **Quote gate** | Dialogue help waits until **who is speaking** and **the situation** are clear — not six proverb lines from a generic voice. |
| **Tagline gate** | Craft feedback stays specific — not a movie-poster moral (*freedom vs duty*). |
| **Six-alternative protocol** | Six complete wordings when you're stuck on a phrase — inspiration to pick, blend, reject, or replace with your own line. |
| **PGM lock** | A fact **frozen in your project files** — edits that contradict it need your explicit OK. |
| **Priority fixes** | In this methodology, means **finish the critique and get options** — not permission to rewrite your chapter. |
| **CH## L##** | How you point at a line in revision — chapter and line number (e.g. CH07 L63). |


---

Methodology without governance becomes ghostwriting with extra steps. This document has two jobs:


| Job            | What you learn                                                             |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Audit**      | How to **diagnose** prose — Signs, developmental letters, filed reports    |
| **Governance** | What may **change** in your manuscript — apply gate, locks, word contracts |


A **rubric alone** is audit. You can name every Sign in a chapter and still lose the book if the tool pastes rewrites while you asked for critique. **Governance** is the rule that diagnosis stays readonly until **you** authorize a specific change — by picking an option, naming a line fix, or typing your own revision.

---

## Audit vs governance


|                          | **Audit** (diagnosis)                                                                                  | **Governance** (authorization)                        |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Asks**                 | What works? What fails? What should we try?                                                            | Who may edit the manuscript, and under what words?    |
| **Typical outputs**      | Developmental letter, tone pass, continuity findings, six word options                                 | Your explicit pick, your line fix, your promoted note |
| **Touches the draft?**   | **No** — reports live beside the manuscript until you act                                              | **Only** what you named in this turn                  |
| **Examples in this doc** | Signs, feedback shape, report corpus, priority order                                                   | Apply gate, author locks, "What your words mean"      |
| **Without the other**    | Rubric with no gate → sneak rewrites (`[06-failure-modes.md](06-failure-modes.md)` — ghostwrite drift) | Gate with no rubric → you authorize changes blind     |


**Short version:** Audit is **what to look at**. Governance is **what may land on the page**.

The sections below are mostly **audit mechanics** — because good diagnosis is long to learn. Governance is shorter on the page but **non-negotiable**: orient → diagnose → options → **apply only on your say-so** (*[Why audit-first](#why-audit-first)*).

## Apply gate in practice

The apply gate is simple to state and easy to violate in chat: **nothing in your manuscript changes until you authorize a specific change.**

### A governed revision on one line

Example beat: hedging on `CH07 L63` — *It seemed the door might have been open.*


| Step | You say                                             | What should happen                                                          | Manuscript                    |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| 1    | "Review this passage."                              | Developmental letter — verdict, numbered notes, no paste-in                 | **Unchanged**                 |
| 2    | "Priority fixes — especially CH07 L63."             | Full critique; if you want options, six **complete** wordings for that line | **Unchanged**                 |
| 3a   | "Use option 3."                                     | Only option 3 applied at `CH07 L63`                                         | **One line**                  |
| 3b   | "Here's my take: *The door stood open.* Orient me." | Questions or convergence on **your** line — not silent apply of option 3    | **Unchanged** until you paste |
| 3c   | "CH07 L63: delete *seemed*."                        | Only that mechanical fix                                                    | **One line**                  |


**Ungoverned (ghostwrite drift):** Step 1 returns a letter **and** a rewritten paragraph already in your file. You asked for audit; you got substitution. See `[06-failure-modes.md](06-failure-modes.md)`.

### Same gate on filed reports

A **continuity check** or **tone audit** is still readonly: findings go to `exploration/` (or your equivalent scratch folder). The report does not edit canon. **You** decide which fixes to make and when — same gate as a chat critique.

### What counts as authorization


| Counts                                                    | Does not count                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Use 3" / "Apply option 3 with minor edit"                | "Fix this chapter" / "Make it better"                         |
| "CH07 L63: [exact change]"                                | Tool inferring "priority fixes" means paste-in rewrite        |
| You pasting your own revision after cluster + author take | Nearest numbered option applied because it was "close enough" |


Move types (pick, blend, cluster + author take): `[02-prosthetic-model.md](02-prosthetic-model.md)`.

## Why audit-first

LLM tools are built to **sound plausible**. Craft often needs **specific, implausible truth** — your voice, your constraints, your continuity.

A governed session usually runs in four beats:

1. **Orient** — your notes, anchor, rubric for this chapter
2. **Diagnose** — critique existing prose, not a fresh draft from the tool
3. **Options** — word choices or questions, if you want them (`[02-prosthetic-model.md](02-prosthetic-model.md)` — six alternatives, pick vs cluster + author take)
4. **Apply** — only what **you** authorize

### When not to audit

You do **not** audit an empty page. **First draft**, exploratory brainstorming, and generative *what if* sessions are for discovery — not for rubric policing. **Audit-first** applies when you have prose you are **protecting** and want diagnosis without substitution. **Audit fatigue** is real: not every paragraph needs a Signs pass on day one. **Governance** still applies whenever something could touch the manuscript — even during brainstorming, nothing lands without your OK.

## Signs — craft vocabulary (starter list)

A Sign is a **name for a pattern** you notice in your draft — sterile cadence, goldilocks diction, hedging. When one matters, you note **which line** and **why**, then decide: revise yourself, ask for word options, or keep the line because it's intentional voice. The rubric **diagnoses**; it does not rewrite for you.

**Where the list lives:** **This repository** documents methodology only — starter categories (table below), not a numbered master checklist. Many long projects settle near **~18 items** after a few revision passes.

**Reference rubric (separate tooling repo):** The numbered list ships in [novelist-plugin](https://github.com/pojoguy/novelist-plugin) — a different repository from this one. See [`prompts/rubric.md`](https://github.com/pojoguy/novelist-plugin/blob/main/prompts/rubric.md) there: Signs 0–18, Human Signals matrix (§3), priority-fixes rules. Section 2 (character voice protocols) belongs in **your** book repo. Clone novelist-plugin only if you want that stack; copy or adapt the rubric into your own project.

**Public reference catalog:** [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) — adapt for fiction POV and voice. **No endorsements are intended or implied** — Wikipedia does not endorse this repository.

Extend for your genre — POV leaks, gesture format, era-locked vocabulary, dialogue tags, and similar.


| Category             | Examples                                            |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Meta / attribution   | "Here is the rewritten scene," tool commentary      |
| Sterile cadence      | Three+ single-sentence paragraphs in a row          |
| Punctuation abuse    | Em-dash as structural crutch                        |
| Emotional telling    | "Fear bloomed," "He realized that…"                 |
| Hedging              | "It seemed," "might have been," "could be argued"   |
| Pompous diction      | utilize, commence, robust                           |
| Goldilocks words     | shimmer, tapestry, luminous, woven, spectral        |
| Generic description  | Abstract nouns where concrete detail earns the line |
| Probabilistic naming | High-frequency LLM fantasy names                    |


**When a Sign fights your intentional voice:** both can be true — **you** decide. Signs are for **your** revision session, not authorship proof or peer policing. Detectors, franchise formula, and replication testing: `[07-ethics-and-transparency.md](07-ethics-and-transparency.md)`.

**When six word options still will not land:** the beat may be **structured wrong** — not a vocabulary problem. Stop line-shopping; rewrite the paragraph or reorder. See `[02-prosthetic-model.md](02-prosthetic-model.md)` — *When six options fail*; `[06-failure-modes.md](06-failure-modes.md)` — *Six-alternative treadmill*.

**Testing adherence on your corpus** (optional, for authors who care about detector debate): [`examples/signs-replication-protocol.md`](../examples/signs-replication-protocol.md).

## What good developmental feedback looks like

When you ask for a developmental pass, you deserve something that reads like a **human editor's letter** — not chat flattery, not a sneak rewrite.

**What you should receive:**

- A short **verdict** — does the passage do its job? What's the main gap?
- **What's already working** — so revision does not sand away strengths
- **Numbered notes**, each tied to a line or quote: what's off, why a reader would care, what **you** might try next
- A **priority order** when several issues compete — so you fix structure before polish
- A clear **close** — especially whether anything was changed in your files without your say-so

**What should not pass for feedback:**

- "Beautiful prose!" then three tiny nits
- Poster taglines (*duty vs freedom; the land always punishes…*) instead of craft direction
- A rewritten paragraph you did not ask to paste in

The fill-in schema for producing that letter lives in `[templates/editorial-recommendations.md](../templates/editorial-recommendations.md)` — useful if you or a collaborator want a consistent report shape.

## Editorial report corpus

One chapter critique is not enough for a novel. Over months you **file** typed reports — trope ledger, tone read, continuity check — and **reload** them when you return to the same span.

Chat forgets. A saved report does not.

### How it works over a long project

```text
Your draft + your notes
        │
        ▼
One diagnostic pass (one question type per file)
        │
        ▼
Save readonly under exploration/ — label the span (e.g. ch1–15)
        │
        ▼
Next session: open prior reports before re-critiquing the same chapters
        │
        ▼
You promote only what you choose → canon notes / manuscript
```

### Why filed reports are worth the habit


| Property                            | What it gives you                                                      |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Readonly**                        | Diagnosis stays separate from the draft — you still apply every change |
| **Typed**                           | One lens per file (trope vs tone vs continuity) — easier to find later |
| **Span-labeled**                    | You know which draft and chapters the pass covered                     |
| **Non-canonical until you promote** | A critique is not story truth until you say so                         |
| **Citable**                         | You can point back: "see tone pass §3 on CH07 L63"                     |


### Report types you might accumulate


| Type                          | Question it answers                                            | You revise prose?                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Developmental pass**        | What works / what fails on this chapter?                       | **Yes** — you draft from the letter                                                      |
| **Trope / subversion ledger** | What genre beats did we set up, refuse, or pay — at what cost? | **Yes** — planning and revision input                                                    |
| **Tone audit**                | Where does voice drift (maudlin, uplift, poster copy)?         | **Yes**                                                                                  |
| **Arc / maturation read**     | Does character change read earned?                             | **Yes**                                                                                  |
| **Relationship audit**        | Does a bond beat violate a metanote or POV choice?             | **Yes**                                                                                  |
| **Timeline audit**            | Does the calendar hold across chapters?                        | **Yes**                                                                                  |
| **Comparanda diagnosis**      | How does our opening compare to quarantined craft references?  | **Yes** — craft only; never paste reference prose                                        |
| **Continuity check**          | Does prose contradict **registered** facts?                    | **No** — readonly compare; you decide fixes. May include a reference ID for your records |


**Developmental** reports are **letters you act on**. **Continuity** reports are **fact compares** against your notes — same apply gate, different job. Habits for continuity checks: `[08-infrastructure-techniques.md](08-infrastructure-techniques.md)`.

You do not need every type on day one. The habit that matters: **run a pass, file it, reload it** — not one-off chat that evaporates.

### Using your corpus


| Habit                                                                      | Why                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Open prior reports before another pass on the same span                    | You build on paid diagnosis instead of starting over   |
| Point at report + line (`CH## L##`) when a note depends on an earlier pass | Traceable reasoning across months                      |
| Treat filed reports as **scratch** until you promote them                  | Critique ≠ canon                                       |
| Keep report text out of the manuscript                                     | Reports **inform** your revision; they do not paste in |

## Author locks

An **author lock** is governance that **outlasts one session** — a rule you set and expect every critique, option set, and continuity pass to respect:

- "This character is never trustworthy as a mentor"
- "Accent marks speaker register — not a typo"
- "This foreword URL breaks immersion — ask before removing it"
- "Calendar: peel is green, not red — locked in my notes"

**Record locks** where you already keep canon — rationale doc, PGM entry, dated inline note. A lock is not a Sign; it is **story law** that audit must not "helpfully" override.

### When a lock meets critique


| Situation                            | Governed response                                                             | Ungoverned failure                              |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Critique suggests breaking a lock    | Note the **conflict** — lock vs suggestion — **you** choose                   | Silent "fix" that rewrites locked fact or voice |
| Continuity check flags prose vs lock | Finding cites lock + line; no auto-edit                                       | Report "corrects" manuscript to generic habit   |
| Six options all violate a lock       | Options should respect locks; if none can, say so — malformed beat or reorder | Options that smuggle a lock break               |


When a lock conflicts with generic critique, **both belong on the table** — you choose; silent "fixes" are not collaboration.

**PGM locks** (facts frozen in project files) are the same habit at scale: edits that contradict registered fact need your explicit OK — same apply gate, stronger memory. Habits: `[08-infrastructure-techniques.md](08-infrastructure-techniques.md)`.

## What your words mean

**Word contracts** are governance for chat: the same English phrase must not mean "rewrite my chapter" in one session and "letter only" in the next.

When you ask for help, these phrases have **specific** meanings in this methodology:


| You say                | What you should get back                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Review this passage"  | Critique only — no manuscript changes                                                                                                                    |
| "Priority fixes"       | Full critique + options where needed — still no paste-in rewrite                                                                                         |
| "Use option 3"         | Only that option applied to the flagged beat — **pick** (`[02-prosthetic-model.md](02-prosthetic-model.md)`)                                             |
| "Here's my take: …"    | Help on **your** line — not silent apply of the nearest numbered option — **cluster + author take** (`[02-prosthetic-model.md](02-prosthetic-model.md)`) |
| "CH07 L63: add period" | Only that named mechanical fix                                                                                                                           |
| "Apply the edit pass"  | Still item-by-item — unless you named a specific line fix                                                                                                |
| "What if we change X between CH04 L39–L40?" | Readonly **ripple discussion** across that span — what breaks, what holds; no apply |
| "Load grounding for this beat before we audit lines" | **Place and material first** — spatial truth before line-level Signs. See [`05-workflow-patterns.md`](05-workflow-patterns.md) — *Grounding capture*, *POV-blind grounding* |


If you get a rewritten scene when you asked for critique, that is **ghostwrite drift**. Named patterns and fixes: [`06-failure-modes.md`](06-failure-modes.md) — or [*Failure shapes — quick recognition*](#failure-shapes--quick-recognition) below.

## Pointing at lines in revision

When you or a collaborator refer to the manuscript, use **chapter + line**:

- `CH07 L63` — chapter 7, line 63
- `CH04 L39–L40` — a range

`L63` alone or `Ch.7` alone get ambiguous across sessions — `CH## L##` is the convention this methodology uses.

**Line numbers drift** after you edit a chapter. Re-point with fresh `CH## L##`, or cite a **short quoted snippet** so the note still lands when counts shift. On indexed long projects, **reanchor** the chapter after substantive edits so locators stay trustworthy — [`05-workflow-patterns.md`](05-workflow-patterns.md) — *Passage reanchor*.

## When many issues land at once

Tackle in this order — structure before lipstick:

1. **Mechanical** — typos, punctuation (when you've named the lines)
2. **Continuity / locked facts** — wrong fact blocks everything else
3. **Structure** — scene order, missing bridge beat
4. **Prose / Signs** — line-level craft
5. **Polish** — rhythm and word choice after the beat holds

Good developmental feedback usually ends with a **suggested order** like this — so you are not polishing prose on a scene that still needs reordering.

## Failure shapes — quick recognition

Named traps that justify audit discipline. **Recognition here; full patterns in** [`06-failure-modes.md`](06-failure-modes.md):

| What you see | What it usually means | Full pattern in `06` |
|--------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Six interchangeable "wise" lines, no speaker | Dialogue before situation is set — **quote gate** | [*Proverb dialogue*](06-failure-modes.md#proverb-dialogue) |
| Critique reads like a movie poster (*duty vs freedom…*) | **Tagline gate** failure — not craft direction | [*Thesis tagline compression*](06-failure-modes.md#thesis-tagline-compression) |
| "Review this passage" → rewritten prose on the page | **Apply gate** violated | [*Ghostwrite drift*](06-failure-modes.md#ghostwrite-drift) |
| Six word options still will not land | **Malformed beat** or line-shopping treadmill | [*Six-alternative treadmill*](06-failure-modes.md#six-alternative-treadmill) |
| *Not A, not B. C.* — sounds decisive, means nothing | **Thin solution space** — forced emission | [*Thin solution space*](06-failure-modes.md#thin-solution-space-retrieval-bias-stall) |
| Two lore facts merged that should stay separate | Continuity load missing — check your notes | [*Wrong causal merge*](06-failure-modes.md#wrong-causal-merge) |

**What protects you:** structure and facts first, readonly critique second, **you** apply last — same two-step habit as [*Apply gate in practice*](#apply-gate-in-practice).
